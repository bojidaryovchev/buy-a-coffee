# Design System: Buy a Coffee

This document is the standard that components are reviewed against. Where it
names a Tailwind class, the class is the specification. Where a component in
the repository disagrees with it, the component is wrong.

Tokens live in `apps/web/src/app/globals.css`. Token names are an interface used
across the storefront and the admin panel: a name is never renamed or removed.

Where this document gives a link's target it names the page, and in brackets
the route in `apps/web/src/lib/routes.ts` (`routes.wizard`). No component
writes a path: every `href` is built by `href(locale, …)`, `categoryHref`,
`productHref` or `brandHref` from that file, so the address carries the locale
and the slug tables decide its spelling. File paths under `app/` are relative
to `apps/web/src/`.

## Overview

**North star: the counter of a well-stocked espresso bar, lit.** The shelf behind
the counter is loud — twenty brands, every pack a different colour. The counter
is dark green under warm light, the walls are cream, a cup steams at one end,
the price list is set in one typeface, and the person behind it asks one
question first: _what machine do you have?_

Three layers carry that, and they must agree:

1. **Navigation is machine first.** The catalog is entered by brewing system and
   by machine model, ahead of brand and ahead of product type.
2. **The interface is quiet; the packs carry the colour.** Warm neutrals, pine as
   the structural anchor, one gold accent, hairlines instead of shadows.
3. **The voice is a plain-spoken advisor.** See `PRODUCT.md`, "Voice".

**Key characteristics**

- Pine green, cream paper, one gold. Clay appears only where a price was reduced.
- Seven brewing-system colours, each one always beside the system's name, on
  our own small drawing of the system's capsule (see "System icon").
- A serif display face for headings, a sans face for everything a customer reads
  to decide: names, prices, facts.
- Squared geometry: radii of 2 to 10 px. No control or container is
  pill-shaped.
- Flat. Structure comes from tone, space and hairlines, used sparingly. Shadows
  are for things that float over the page, and for the pack that stands on it.
- Every figure a customer compares is tabular and carries its unit.
- Packshots are cut out and stand on their well with a soft contact shadow. A
  pack not yet cut out sits on pure white, as it was photographed (see
  "Imagery").
- One drawn motif, the curl from the mark, and a small set of our own drawings
  — beans, steam, a cup, crema, a cup ring, a spill. Never a photograph of
  them.
- Motion marks arrival, feedback and progress, and never costs a Web Vitals
  budget (see "Motion").

**What was wrong before, and what this fixes.** The first build had no imagery
on the home page and read closer to a law firm than a coffee shop; the logo was
gold and brown and shared no colour with a green-and-grey interface; and the
catalog was organised by product type when customers shop by the machine they
own. The identity — pine, cream, Literata over Inter, small radii — was coherent
and stays.

**What was still wrong in October, and what Phase K fixes.** A design review
after launch found the system sound and three things that read as cheap before
any styling does: packshots on their own light-grey photo grounds, which drew a
grey rectangle inside every white well and left packs at random scales; a
bordered box around nearly everything, so nothing stood out; and no material —
no light, texture or depth, nothing on the home page that said coffee except
the words. Phase K (`IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md`) cuts the packs out, takes most of
the boxes away, and adds the material of a cup of coffee — drawn, light and
fast.

The hues are chosen to be distinct from each other and from pine (165), gold
(78), clay (40) and critical (27). They are not the system owners' brand
colours and are not meant to be.

**Using them.** A system id arrives at runtime, and Tailwind cannot generate a
class assembled from one. Put `data-system={system.id}` on the element instead.
`globals.css` then sets two custom properties on it and its children:

- `--system` — the solid. Use as `text-(--system)`, `bg-(--system)`,
  `border-(--system)`.
- `--system-wash` — the wash. Use as `bg-(--system-wash)`.

An unknown id resolves to `ink-700` on `paper-sunken`, so a system added before
it has a colour still renders legibly.

Sanctioned uses of a system colour, and no others:

| Use                                    | Foreground     | Background      | Lowest measured ratio |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------- | --------------- | --------------------- |
| System badge                           | `--system`     | `--system-wash` | 6.39 (Caffitaly)      |
| System icon (a graphic: 3:1 needed)    | `--system`     | wash or paper   | 6.39 (Caffitaly)      |
| System name as text on a page or card  | `--system`     | any paper tone  | 6.41 (Caffitaly)      |

**The Drawing Tone Rule.** A drawing tone is never text, never a control, never
a border and never a meaning. It has no rows in the contrast table for the same
reason the brand logos have none: nothing is read from it.

### Named rules

**The One Gold Rule.** Gold is a fill under `ink-900`, used for the single most
important action in a viewport. At most one `gold-500` control is visible at a
time. Gold is never text on a paper tone: `gold-500` on `paper` measures 2.85:1.
On `pine-900` it may be text, as `gold-300` (8.09:1). On `espresso-900` it may
be text as itself (`gold-500`, 6.00:1) or as `gold-300` (9.82:1).

**The Gold Ground Rule.** The accent button sits on `pine-900` (edge 4.95:1),
on `espresso-900` (edge 6.00:1) or on `paper-raised` (edge 3.07:1). It never
sits directly on `paper` or `paper-sunken`, where its edge falls under 3:1.

**The Clay Means Reduced Rule.** Clay marks a price that went down: the reduced
price, the reduction badge, the promotions link and band. It is not a caution
colour, a brand flourish or a hover state. Cautions use `caution`.

**The Critical Stays In Forms Rule.** `critical` appears on a form error and a
destructive admin action. "Изчерпан" is not an error and is a neutral badge.
This keeps red and clay from ever sitting side by side on a product.

**The Named Colour Rule.** A system colour never appears without the system's
name in text beside it. A system icon alone, a coloured border alone, a legend
somewhere else on the page: none of these is enough.

**The Cut-out Rule.** A pack that has an approved cut-out stands on its well
with a contact shadow; a pack that has none sits on `well` exactly as it was
photographed, because any tinted ground draws a rectangle around a photograph.
See "Imagery". This replaces the White Well Rule, which held while no pack was
cut out.

**The Measured Contrast Rule.** A text and background pairing is allowed only if
it is in the table below. Adding one means measuring it and adding the row.
Opacity is not used to lighten text; use the next ink step.

### Measured contrast

Method: each OKLCH value is converted to linear sRGB, clipped to gamut (no token
is out of gamut), encoded to 8-bit sRGB, and the WCAG 2.x relative-luminance
contrast ratio is computed from the 8-bit values. Ratios are truncated to two
decimals, never rounded up. The 8-bit values were checked against what Chromium
paints for every token (41 of 41 within one step per channel). `a/NN/b` means
`a` at NN% alpha composited over `b`.

Required: 4.5 for text, 3 for the edge of a control or a graphic that carries
meaning. Rows marked PROHIBITED and LEGACY fail on purpose and are listed so
that nobody measures them again; see "Migration" at the end.

## Typography

**Display:** Literata, weight 600, `letter-spacing: -0.015em`, `text-wrap:
balance`. Headings only: `h1`, `h2`, `h3` in page chrome, panel titles, wizard
option labels. Loaded with its optical-size axis and drawn with
`font-optical-sizing: auto`, so every heading gets the design made for its
size: the display cut, finer and higher in contrast, at 40 px and up; the text
cut in a 20 px panel title. Only the weights in use are loaded.

**Accent:** Literata italic, weight 500, for one phrase on a page: the hero
title's last words, in `gold-300` on `pine-900`. Nowhere else, never on paper,
never on a whole sentence.

**Body:** Inter. Everything a customer reads to decide: product names, prices,
facts, labels, controls, paragraphs.

Both carry Cyrillic. That is a requirement, not a preference.

### Named rules

**The Sans For Deciding Rule.** Product names and prices are Inter. Product
names mix Cyrillic, Latin and numerals in one string ("Капсули Nespresso Rema
Caffè Arabica Gold 10 бр.") and run to three lines on a phone; a serif at 13 px
does not survive that. Literata is for the shop's own voice — headings.

**The Tabular Rule.** Every price, price per cup, price per kilogram, count,
intensity numeral and pack size is `tabular-nums`.

**The Unit Rule.** A figure is never shown without its unit or its scale:
"5,60 €", "0,35 € на чаша", "39,80 € / кг", "8 от 12", "16 бр.", "1 кг".

**The Eleven Pixel Floor Rule.** Nothing is set below `text-2xs` (11 px).

**The Sixteen Pixel Field Rule.** Form fields use `text-input` (16 px). Below
that, iOS Safari zooms the page when the field takes focus.

**The No Uppercase Sentences Rule.** Uppercase is for labels of one to three
words. A reason phrase, a caveat or a system name is never uppercased. System
and brand names keep their own capitalisation everywhere.

**The Heading Order Rule.** One `h1` per page. Levels are never skipped. A
product name in a grid is an `h3` when the grid has an `h2` above it and an
`h2` when it does not.

## Layout

**Shell.** `.shell`: max width 1180 px (`--container-shell`), centred, side
padding 16 px below `md` and 32 px from `md`. Every band's content sits in a
shell; band backgrounds run edge to edge.

**Breakpoints.** Tailwind defaults: `sm` 640, `md` 768, `lg` 1024, `xl` 1280.
Designs are specified at 390 px (phone) and 1366 px (desktop) and must not
scroll horizontally at 320 px. Every page is checked at 320, 360, 390, 414,
768, 1024, 1280 and 1440 px (see "Checking a layout" below). Below `lg` a
screen is assumed to be touched: that is where the touch sizes below apply.

**Vertical rhythm.**

| Use                        | Value                                      |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Section padding            | `py-12` (48 px), `md:py-16` (64 px)        |
| Hero padding               | `py-10`, `md:py-16`                        |
| Section title to content   | `mb-6` (24 px)                             |
| Between panels in a column | `gap-6` below `md`, `gap-8` from `md`      |
| Inside a panel             | `p-4` below `md`, `p-5` or `p-6` from `md` |
| Inside a product card body | `p-3` below `md`, `p-4` from `md`          |

Spacing uses the 4 px Tailwind scale. No arbitrary pixel values for spacing.

**Bands.** Sections alternate by tone, not by box: `paper` by default,
`paper-sunken` for the wizard entry and the footer, `paper-raised` for the
newsletter band, `pine-900` for the hero,
`espresso-900` for the Vending Zone band, `clay-100` for promotions. Adjacent
bands of the same tone are separated by a `border-t border-line`. Two dark
bands never stack.

**The frame's foot.** The space between a page and what follows it is the
frame's: `mt-20` above the newsletter band and the footer, which sit together
in one wrapper (`data-frame-foot`). A page whose last section is a full-bleed
band of its own (the home page's Vending Zone, marked `data-ends-on-band`)
meets the frame edge to edge instead (`globals.css`).

**Grids.**

| Grid                              | Columns                      | Gap                                        |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Product grid, full width          | 2, `md:` 3, `lg:` 4          | `gap-x-3 gap-y-6`, `md:gap-x-5 md:gap-y-8` |
| Product grid beside a filter rail | 2, `md:` 3, `lg:` 3, `xl:` 3 | same                                       |
| System tiles                      | 2, `md:` 3, `lg:` 4          | `gap-3`, `md:gap-4`                        |
| Product page top                  | 1, `lg:` 2 equal columns     | `gap-8`, `lg:gap-12`                       |
| Listing                           | 1, `lg:` `240px 1fr`         | `lg:gap-8`                                 |

Grid children are `li` elements with `h-full` cards, so every card in a row is
the height of the tallest.

**Measure.** Running text is capped at `--container-measure` (68ch). Lead
paragraphs at 60ch.

**The Reserved Space Rule.** Anything that arrives late or may be absent keeps
its box: images carry an aspect ratio, optional card rows keep their height,
buttons keep their width while pending. Layout shift is a defect with a budget
(CLS under 0.1).

**The Min Height Rule.** Controls are sized with `min-h-*` and padding, not a
fixed `h-*`, so enlarged text grows the control instead of overflowing it.

**The Touch Target Rule.** Below `lg`, everything a finger is meant to hit —
a button, a chip, a field, a filter row, a footer link, a breadcrumb step, a
link that stands on a line of its own — is at least 44 px tall: `min-h-11`,
and its desktop size from `lg` (`lg:min-h-9`, `lg:min-h-10`). A link inside
running text is exempt (it is part of the sentence). Where a standalone
inline link cannot grow without moving the text around it (a heading's link,
a link in a table cell, the announcement bar's phone number), it takes
vertical padding instead (`py-2` to `py-3.5`): on an inline box padding
widens what can be hit without moving a line. Not where two such links sit
on neighbouring lines: their padded boxes would overlap and the lower would
take the upper's taps.

**The Unbroken Label Rule.** A control's label never wraps: buttons and chips
are `whitespace-nowrap`. Where a label is longer than the room it is given,
the control grows, or the layout changes around it — it stacks, takes the
full width, or (for a label that names a count and a system, „Вижте всички
35 продукта за Lavazza A Modo Mio“) becomes a text link, which may wrap —
never the text. Below `sm` a lone full-width action keeps only 12 px of side
padding (`px-3 sm:px-6` on size `lg`), which is what lets „Намерете кафе за
вашата машина“ fit at 320 px.

**The Whole Number Rule.** A phone number is one unit: every `tel:` link is
`white-space: nowrap` (`globals.css`, base layer), in running text too —
„+359 897 943 / 424“ across two lines reads as two numbers.

**The Meta Rule.** Meta text beside a label — a count, a price, a state —
never gets a column of its own and never breaks inside (`whitespace-nowrap`).
Where it goes is decided by the width of the thing it sits in, not the
window: the card is a size container (`@container`), and from `@md` (28rem of
card) the note sits to the right of the label, level with its first line,
while the label wraps beside it; narrower, it goes under the label on its own
line. The same card is full width on a phone and half a two-column grid on a
laptop, so a window breakpoint would get one of the two wrong. The wizard's
options and toggles and the newsletter form follow it.

**Checking a layout.** A throwaway Playwright script loads every page type at
the eight widths above and reports, for each: horizontal scroll and the
element that causes it; text clipped or sticking out of its box; a control
whose label wraps; a short label or count that breaks; a target under 44 px
below `lg`; an image drawn at another aspect ratio than its own; text that
collides with other text. It also saves the screenshots, which are looked at:
a measurement finds a cut-off „12 от / 13“, not a page that reads badly.

## Elevation

Flat by default. Three devices, in order of preference:

1. **Tone.** `paper-sunken` → `paper` → `paper-raised`.
2. **Hairline.** `border border-line` around cards and panels; `border-line-strong`
   around controls.
3. **Shadow**, only for things that sit above the page:

| Token          | Value                                          | Use                                                                  |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `shadow-raise` | `0 1px 2px oklch(0.22 0.025 60 / 0.07)`        | A product card or tile on hover and focus-within. Nothing at rest.   |
| `shadow-float` | `0 8px 28px -12px oklch(0.22 0.025 60 / 0.24)` | Dropdown panel, typeahead list, mobile drawer, filter sheet, dialog. |

Overlays that cover the page (drawer, sheet, dialog) add a scrim of
`bg-ink-900/40`.

Z-order: sticky header 40; dropdown and typeahead 50; drawer, sheet and dialog
50 with their scrim; skip link 100.

**The contact shadow.** A cut-out pack standing on its well casts a soft
elliptical shadow at its foot: a radial gradient of `ink-900` at up to 30 %,
about a seventh of the pack's width tall, with no edge. It is part of the
picture, not elevation, and it is the only shadow at rest. On a dark ground it
is black at up to 55 %.

**The No Resting Shadow Rule.** Nothing on the page has a shadow until the
customer interacts with it, except a pack's contact shadow.

**The No Nested Cards Rule.** A bordered panel does not go inside a bordered
panel. Use a hairline divider or a tone step.

| Token        | Radius | Use                                                                        |
| ------------ | ------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `rounded-xs` | 2 px   | Badges, the system badge, checkboxes, step numerals, intensity track ends. |
| `rounded-sm` | 4 px   | Buttons, inputs, selects, chips, navigation rows, thumbnails.              |

- Borders are 1 px. The only 2 px strokes are the focus ring, the selected

- Icons are 1.5 to 2 px stroked line drawings on a 16, 20 or 24 px grid, in

  move.

## Motion

Motion marks three things and nothing else: **arrival** (a pack set down, a
section coming into view — once), **feedback** (what the customer did took
effect) and **progress** (a wait is being worked on). One loop is ambient — the
steam — and it stops whenever nobody can see it.

| Kind       | What moves                                                                                                    | How                                                                                 |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| State      | Colour, border and background of a control                                                                    | `transition-colors`, 150 ms                                                         |
| Overlay    | Drawer and sheet                                                                                              | translate and opacity, 200 ms, `ease-out`                                           |
| Hover      | A pack rises 5 px while its shadow widens and softens; a capsule drawing tips 10°. On devices that hover only | CSS transition, 280 ms, inside `@media (hover: hover)`                              |
| Arrival    | The hero's packs are set down, beans drop, steam starts — on load, once, within 1.2 s                         | CSS keyframes                                                                       |
| Arrival    | Tiles and drawings below the fold rise into place as they enter the viewport                                  | CSS scroll-driven animation (`animation-timeline: view()`), inside `@supports`      |
| Feedback   | A bean arcs from „Добави“ to the header cart, which nudges and counts up                                      | Web Animations API, started after the cart has changed                              |
| Feedback   | The product page's intensity track fills segment by segment, the first time it is seen                        | CSS scroll-driven animation                                                         |
| Feedback   | The cup fills and steams when an order is sent                                                                | CSS keyframes                                                                       |
| Progress   | The home page's ordering-steps curl draws itself as it crosses the middle of the screen                       | `stroke-dashoffset`, scroll-driven                                                  |
| Ambient    | Steam over a cup                                                                                              | CSS keyframes, paused off-screen                                                    |
| Navigation | A card's pack becomes the product page's photograph                                                           | View Transitions API, behind Next's `viewTransition` flag; elsewhere the page swaps |

Where `animation-timeline` is not supported (Firefox, today) the element is
simply in place: scroll-driven motion is an enhancement, never a condition for
seeing anything. A scroll-driven element starts from at least 15 % opacity,
never from nothing.

**Never.** A carousel or marquee that moves by itself, parallax, scroll
hijacking or a smooth-scroll library, numbers that count up, scrambled text, a
cursor follower, a video or Lottie hero, an image that zooms, and an animation
library. Everything here is CSS, the Web Animations API or the View Transitions
API, and costs no JavaScript on the first load but the few lines that switch
scroll-driven motion on after it ("The Quiet Load Rule").

### The Web Vitals rules

Each rule names what it protects. They hold for every motion on every page.

- **The Still LCP Rule** (LCP). A page's likely LCP element never starts
  invisible: Chrome ignores a paint at zero opacity, so a fade-in delays LCP by
  its own length. Those elements are the hero title and lead, the tallest hero
  pack, the first card photo of a listing and the product gallery photo. They
  may move by transform; they never fade.
- **The Drawn Decoration Rule** (LCP). Decoration is inline SVG or CSS: no
  request, and never an LCP candidate. A raster decoration, if one is ever
  added, is lazy, below the fold, `fetchpriority="low"`, in a box of fixed size.
- **The Compositor Rule** (CLS, INP). Only `transform` and `opacity` are
  animated, and `stroke-dashoffset` on a drawing of a few hundred pixels.
  Never `width`, `height`, `top`, `left`, `margin` or `padding`: layout shift
  counts moved layout, and a transform moves none.
- **The No Motion Script Above The Fold Rule** (INP, LCP). Motion in the first
  screen is CSS. JavaScript motion starts on an interaction, after the state it
  reports has been committed, and imports nothing.
- **The Quiet Load Rule** (LCP, FCP). Scroll-driven motion starts after the
  page has loaded: every `animation-timeline: view()` rule waits for
  `:root[data-motion]`, which `MotionGate` sets on the first idle moment
  after `load` (`test/motion-css.test.ts` holds every such rule to it). A
  `view()` timeline present while the page loads put the home page's LCP
  back by about 500 ms, and a listing's first paint by 100 to 160 ms, on the
  budget's phone; switched on after load it costs neither. For the same
  reason a scroll-driven effect is never repeated across a listing: an
  intensity track pours on the product page only, and is simply full on a
  card.
- **The Unseen Loop Rule** (INP, battery). The steam pauses when it leaves the
  viewport (an `IntersectionObserver` sets `animation-play-state`) and when the
  tab is hidden. No other loop exists.
- **The Instant Not Missing Rule** (access). Under `prefers-reduced-motion:
reduce` the global rule in `globals.css` stops every animation, and
  JavaScript checks the same media query: everything jumps to its end state.
  The cart still counts up, the confirmation still appears, the steam is drawn
  still.
- **The Brief Will-change Rule** (memory). `will-change` is set when an
  animation starts and removed when it ends; never in a stylesheet for an
  element at rest.
- **The Measured Rule.** `pnpm measure:budgets` runs before and after every
  change that adds motion or imagery, against the same build and data, and the
  numbers go in "Budgets".

## Imagery

Three kinds of image, and no others.

**1. Packshots.** The product's own photographs, the only photographs on the
storefront.

- **Cut out.** Each photograph is cut out to transparency when it is imported
  (`tools/cutout`): two background-removal models whose masks are combined, a
  shape check that fills a cut reaching deep into a pack (a pack is solid; a
  picture printed on a box fools the models), and the white edge taken out. A
  cut-out goes live by itself when the two models agree on 99 % of its pixels
  or more and nothing had to be filled; anything else waits for a person in
  the admin, and a rejected cut-out is never made again.
- **Standing.** A cut-out stands on its well, trimmed to its own edges, at one
  scale for every pack in a grid, with its contact shadow (see "Elevation").
  The well is `well` fading to `paper-sunken` at its foot, so the pack has
  something to stand on; on a dark ground it stands on the ground itself.
- **The fallback.** A pack with no approved cut-out sits on `well` exactly as
  photographed, as before. Both treatments sit in the same grid and keep the
  same box: which one a pack gets never moves anything.

**2. Our drawings.** Inline SVG in the drawing tones, `aria-hidden`, a few
hundred bytes each.

| Drawing    | Where                                                                      |
| ---------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The curl   | Steam over every cup; the line joining the home page's ordering steps      |
| Beans      | Around the hero's packs; into the cart; beside the beans shelf             |
| A cup      | The hero; „order sent“                                                     |
| Crema      | The wizard's entry and its result                                          |
| A cup ring | The 404 page                                                               |
| A spill    | Empty states: an empty cart, a search with nothing found, an empty listing |
| Capsules   | Our capsule diagrams and system icons, unchanged (see "System icon")       |

**The curl** is the mark's own: the crease of the bean in the logo rising into
steam. It is the shop's one graphic device.

**3. Brand logos,** unchanged (see "Brand logo").

**Never.** A photograph of beans, cups, coffee or people; stock or generated
imagery; a drawing that stands in for a product, shows a brand, or shows a
system's capsule other than through our diagrams.

## Components

Each component lists its anatomy, tokens, states, phone behaviour, and what it
must never do. "Phone" means below `md` (768 px).

### Buttons

`Button` and `ButtonLink` in `primitives.tsx`. One shape, six variants.

**Shape.** `inline-flex items-center justify-center gap-2 rounded-sm font-medium
whitespace-nowrap transition-colors`. Icon, when present, is 16 px and precedes
the label. The label never wraps (see "The Unbroken Label Rule").

| Size | Min height                            | Padding             | Type        | Use                                         |
| ---- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| `sm` | `min-h-11` 44 px, `lg:min-h-10` 40 px | `px-3 py-1.5`       | `text-sm`   | Section actions, toolbar, the card control. |
| `md` | `min-h-11` 44 px                      | `px-5 py-2.5`       | `text-base` | Default.                                    |
| `lg` | `min-h-12` 48 px                      | `px-3 sm:px-6 py-3` | `text-base` | Hero and order form.                        |

| Variant     | Rest                                                     | Hover                                | Use                                                          |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `primary`   | `bg-pine-900 text-paper`                                 | `bg-pine-700`                        | The main action of a section on a light ground.              |
| `accent`    | `bg-gold-500 text-ink-900 font-semibold`                 | `bg-gold-600`; on pine `bg-gold-300` | The single most important action in a viewport. New variant. |
| `secondary` | `border border-line-strong bg-paper-raised text-ink-900` | `bg-paper-sunken`                    | Alternative actions; the card's add-to-cart control.         |
| `ghost`     | `text-ink-700`                                           | `bg-paper-sunken text-ink-900`       | Tertiary actions, icon buttons.                              |
| `danger`    | `bg-critical text-paper`                                 | `bg-critical` at 90% opacity         | Destructive admin actions only.                              |
| `on-pine`   | `border border-pine-200 text-paper`                      | `bg-pine-700`                        | The secondary action on a `pine-900` band.                   |

`on-pine` is the secondary action on `pine-900`, as a named variant rather than
something `secondary` turns into inside `.on-pine`: a white card can sit on a
pine band, and its secondary button must stay dark on white.

**States.**

- _Focus:_ the global ring — 2 px `pine-700`, 2 px offset. Inside `.on-pine` it
  is `gold-300`. Never removed.
- _Active:_ same as hover. No scale.
- _Disabled:_ `bg-paper-sunken text-ink-500 cursor-not-allowed` (6.24:1), with
  its edge an inset ring in `line` rather than a border, so a button that goes
  disabled while its form submits keeps its exact width. Not opacity. Prefer not rendering a control that
  can never be used.
- _Pending:_ the label changes to "Изпраща се…", `aria-busy="true"`, the button
  is disabled and keeps its width (`min-w-40` on the order button; on the
  newsletter button both labels are laid out in one grid cell and one is
  `invisible`, so the button is as wide as the longer of the two).

**Phone.** A lone primary or accent action is `w-full`. Two actions stack, the
main one first.

**Never.** Two `accent` buttons in one viewport. An `accent` button on `paper`
or `paper-sunken`. A label on two lines. A button without a text label, unless
it has `aria-label` and is at least 40 by 40 px (44 by 44 below `lg`). A `<div>` or `<span>` acting as a button. A link
styled as a button that performs an action, or the reverse.

### Inputs

**Anatomy.** Label above, field, then helper or error text below.

- _Label:_ `mb-1 block text-sm font-medium text-ink-900`. A required field adds
  `<span aria-hidden class="text-critical"> *</span>` and the field carries
  `required` — for assistive technology, which announces it; the form's
  `noValidate` keeps the browser from acting on it (see "Validation").
- _Field:_ `min-h-12 w-full rounded-sm border border-line-strong bg-paper-raised
px-3 text-input text-ink-900 placeholder:text-ink-300`. A secondary field
  inside a disclosure may be `min-h-11`. `textarea` adds `py-2`.
- _Disclosure for optional fields_ (the order form's „Добавете имейл или
  бележка (по избор)“): a `summary` in `text-sm text-pine-700`, `min-h-11`,
  led by a 16 px chevron that turns a quarter when open — a disclosure says
  that it opens. Its label may take two lines in a narrow column; „(по избор)“
  stays whole.
- _Helper:_ `mt-1 text-xs text-ink-500`.
- _Select:_ the native element with the same classes. No custom listbox.
- _Checkbox as a link_ (filters): an 18 px box, `rounded-xs border
border-line-strong bg-paper-raised`; checked `border-pine-900 bg-pine-900
text-paper` with a 2.5 px check mark.

**States.**

- _Hover:_ `border-ink-500`.
- _Focus:_ `border-pine-700` and the global focus ring. `outline-none` is not
  used on a field.
- _Invalid:_ `aria-invalid="true"`, `border-critical`, and beneath it
  `<p id="…-error" class="mt-1 text-sm text-critical">` tied by
  `aria-describedby` (`FieldError`, `components/forms/field-error.tsx`). The
  message says what to do: "Въведете телефонен номер с поне 9 цифри."
- _Disabled:_ `bg-paper-sunken text-ink-500`.

**Validation.** Every form a person fills in — the cart's order form (the only
order form), the contact form and the enquiry forms built on
it, the newsletter band, the unsubscribe button and the admin panel's forms —
works the same way
(`components/forms/use-schema-form.ts`):

- _One schema per form,_ in `lib/forms/schemas.ts` (storefront) or
  `lib/forms/admin-schemas.ts` (panel). The server action validates every
  submission with it, and the form validates with the same object in the
  browser (react-hook-form with `@hookform/resolvers/zod`). A rule and its
  message exist once; a message reads the same whichever side found the
  problem.
- _No browser bubbles._ The `<form>` carries `noValidate`. `type`, `required`
  and `maxLength` stay on the fields for the keyboard, assistive technology and
  typing, but the browser never stops a submission or shows its own message,
  in its own language and style, ahead of ours.
- _On the press._ Pressing the button (or Enter) checks the whole form. Every
  invalid field shows its message beneath it, the result line says „Поправете
  отбелязаните полета и изпратете отново.“ (the newsletter, with one field,
  shows the field's message there), focus moves to the first invalid field in
  page order — opening the order form's disclosure when that is where it is —
  and nothing is sent. After that first press a field re-checks as it is
  corrected, so a message goes away the moment it is fixed. Nothing is checked
  while a person is still typing for the first time.
- _The server decides._ A valid form is posted to its server action, which
  validates again. Its field messages land under the same fields, and focus
  moves to them; a refusal of the whole request (the rate limit, a product
  that is gone, a failed send) goes in the form's result line. What was typed
  stays in the fields.
- _Without JavaScript_ the form is a plain POST to the same action. The page
  comes back drawn from the action's answer: the same messages under the same
  fields, `aria-invalid` set, the disclosure open if the error is inside it, and
  what was typed put back in the fields.
- _Messages_ are Bulgarian, polite plural, and say what to do: „Въведете…“,
  „Напишете…“, „Изберете…“, „Съкратете … до N знака.“ A field the page fills
  in itself (a product, a token) can only be wrong in a hand-made post, and its
  message says to reload. The honeypot is never checked in the browser: a bot
  that fills it is answered by the server as if it had succeeded.
- _Pending:_ from the press until the answer the button is disabled and
  `aria-busy`, and a second press sends nothing (see Buttons, Pending).

**Phone.** Fields are full width and stack. `type`, `inputMode` and
`autoComplete` are always set (`tel`, `email`, `name`).

**Placeholders** are Bulgarian and say what to type („Вашият имейл адрес“).
An example of a format starts with „Напр.“ („Напр. 0888 123 456“), so it is
never mistaken for a value already filled in.

**Never.** A placeholder used as the label, or one in English. An error shown
by colour alone. A field below 16 px text. Validation that exists only in the
browser. A browser validation bubble. A rule written twice, once for the
browser and once for the server. A message that only says something is wrong
(„Невалиден телефон“) instead of what to do.

### Chips

Three kinds, all `ChipLink` in `primitives.tsx` — no page writes a chip's
classes by hand. All: `inline-flex items-center gap-1.5 rounded-sm border
text-sm whitespace-nowrap`, `min-h-11` (44 px) below `lg` and `lg:min-h-9`
(36 px) from it, `px-3`. A chip is one unit: its label and count never wrap
inside it; a row of chips wraps between chips, or scrolls.

| Kind          | Rest                                                                         | Hover                         | Selected                                                        |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Choice        | `border-line bg-paper-raised text-ink-900 font-medium`                       | `border-pine-500`             | `border-pine-900 bg-pine-900 text-paper`, `aria-current="page"` |
| Active filter | `border-line-strong bg-paper-raised text-ink-700`                            | `border-ink-900 text-ink-900` | —                                                               |
| Answer        | as active filter, with a `label` prefix in `text-2xs uppercase text-ink-500` | same                          | —                                                               |

- A choice chip is a link to another listing, or to a section of the page: a
  system under "Капсули", a machine model on a product page, a system's
  section on a landing. A count follows the label as `text-2xs tabular-nums
text-ink-300` (`text-pine-200` when selected), with what it counts in
  `sr-only` (`countLabel`, " продукта"). A system's icon, 16 px (see
  "System icon"), goes before the label (`leading`), with `data-system` on
  the chip.
- An active-filter chip is a link that removes one filter. It ends in a 12 px
  "×" icon and `<span class="sr-only">— премахни филтъра</span>`.
- An answer chip (wizard) links back to the question that set it and ends in
  `<span class="sr-only">— промяна на отговора</span>`.

**Phone.** Chips wrap. A row of chips longer than two lines — the systems
under "Капсули", a landing's sections, the wizard's answers — becomes a single
scrolling row running to the screen's edges: `relative -mx-4 flex snap-x
scroll-px-4 overflow-x-auto px-4`, each chip `shrink-0 snap-start`. The
scroll padding is what keeps the first chip off the edge: without it the
snap pulls the first chip flush against the screen. `relative` contains the
chips' `sr-only` text, which would otherwise widen the page. The selected chip
is scrolled into view by being first in source order.

**Never.** A hover that turns a chip red: removing a filter is not destructive.
A chip that is not a link or a button. A system chip without the system's name.

### System badge

Names the brewing system a product belongs to. The most important label in the
shop: it answers "does this go in my machine?"

**Anatomy.** `<span data-system={id}>`: the system's icon, then the system's
name.

```
[◘ Dolce Gusto]
```

- _Container:_ `inline-flex max-w-full items-center rounded-xs
bg-(--system-wash) px-1.5 text-(--system)`, `gap-1` at `sm` and `gap-1.5` at
  `md`.
- _Icon:_ `SystemIcon` (see "System icon"), `aria-hidden`.
- _Name:_ `truncate font-semibold`, the system's `name` from
  `BREWING_SYSTEMS`, in its own capitalisation. Never uppercased.

| Size | Min height | Type       | Icon  | Where                                        |
| ---- | ---------- | ---------- | ----- | -------------------------------------------- |
| `sm` | 20 px      | `text-2xs` | 12 px | Product card, typeahead row, recommendation. |
| `md` | 24 px      | `text-xs`  | 16 px | Product page, facts table, listing header.   |

„Lavazza A Modo Mio“) truncate there, as they did beside the old square.

**States.** Static on a card (the card is already one link). On the product
page and in the facts table it is a link to the system's listing: hover
underlines the name, focus shows the global ring. The link is a 44 px target
below `lg` while the badge keeps its 24 px: the link is the badge's
transparent frame, `min-h-11 lg:min-h-6` on the product page's brand line
(`target="grow"`), and in a table cell, where growing would push the value
away from its label, `py-2.5 -my-2.5`, which hands the height back to the
layout (`target="pad"`).

**Data.** The system is resolved from the product's categories through
`BREWING_SYSTEMS` (`categorySlugs` and `categorySourceKeys`). A product that
resolves to no system shows no badge; the row keeps its height on a card.

**Phone.** Same. It truncates with an ellipsis before it wraps.

**Never.** The icon or the colour without the name. A system owner's logo,
logotype or trade dress. The word "оригинални". A system badge on a product
whose system was guessed from its name.

### System icon

`SystemIcon` in `components/catalog/system-icon.tsx`; the shapes in
`components/catalog/system-icons.ts`. The mark before a brewing system's name,
wherever a system is named as a system: the header's „Капсули по система“
list and the mobile drawer's, the system badge, system chips (a category's
subcategories, a landing's sections, a brand page's shelves), the system
filter options and the system's active-filter chip, and the home page's
system tiles.

**What it is.** Our own drawing of the system's capsule, never the system
owner's mark. Most capsules in a system's section are made by others to fit
it; Nestlé's or Lavazza's logo beside them would claim an endorsement
(`PRODUCT.md`). The shape is computed from the outlines the machine finder
draws (`components/wizard/capsule-outlines.ts`, in millimetres, with their
sources), so the icon and the "recognise your capsule" drawing are the same
capsule:

- A capsule is its side silhouette, lid up, scaled to the icon's 20-unit width
  with its own proportions: Nespresso deep and tapering, Dolce Gusto with its
  stepped base and outlet, A Modo Mio shallow, Caffitaly with its groove,
  Lavazza Blue a tub with a flat base.
- The ESE pod is its view from above, the view that tells it apart: the
  pressed coffee as a disc at its 44 mm, the paper edge as a ring at its own
  radius.
- Beans have no outline; their icon is a coffee bean leaning right, its crease
  cut out, drawn in the same manner.

**Derivation.** On a 20 × 16 grid, one unit a pixel at the 16 px size. A rim
(0.3 to 0.6 units in the data) is thickened downwards to one unit so it is
seen; Caffitaly's groove is a one-unit cut through the body; points the eye
cannot place at this size are dropped (Douglas–Peucker, 0.3 units). Widths,
heights and the run of every wall are the outline's;
`test/system-icons.test.ts` checks each icon's height to width against its
capsule's.

**Colour.** Solid, `fill-(--system)`: the system's solid on its wash (the
badge, the tile) or on paper (a row, a chip), 6.39:1 at the lowest
(Caffitaly), against the 3:1 a graphic needs. `data-system` on the element
that holds the icon and the name sets it, as for the badge.

**Sizes.** Height (the width is five fourths of it, `ICON_BOX`):

| Size | Height | Where                                                               |
| ---- | ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `sm` | 12 px  | The small system badge: a card, a typeahead row, a wizard result.   |
| `md` | 16 px  | Rows, chips, filter options and chips, the `md` badge. The default. |
| `lg` | 32 px  | A home page system tile, above its name.                            |

Sixteen pixels is where the seven are told apart at 1x by shape before
colour; at 12 the nozzle, the groove and the ring still show, and below that
Nespresso, Lavazza Blue and Dolce Gusto run together. The 6 to 8 px the old
squares took is too small for any shape. In a row the icon is centred on the
name, so rows of different capsules line up.

**Never.** The system owner's logo, logotype, capsule artwork or trade dress
in its place. An icon without the system's name beside it, or as a link's only
content. An icon for a brand: Caffitaly the system gets the icon, Caffitaly
the brand its logo (see "Brand logo"). A shape redrawn by hand: change the
outline, with its source, and both drawings follow.

### Intensity scale

The source states intensity on five scales — out of 5, 9, 10, 12 and 13 —
depending on the brand. `parseIntensity()` in `lib/catalog/attributes.ts`
returns `{ value, max, fraction }`.

**The rule the component exists for:** a customer must never be able to read
"8" on two cards and conclude they are equally strong. So the component always
shows the scale's own maximum, twice — once as a count of segments and once as
text.

**Anatomy.** A segmented track and a numeral.

```
▮▮▮▮▮▮▮▮▯▯▯▯  8 от 12          ▮▮▮▮▮▮▮▮▯▯  8 от 10
```

- _Track:_ a flex row of exactly `max` segments, `gap-px`, each `flex-1 h-1.5`.
  The first `value` segments are `bg-pine-700`; the rest are `bg-line`. First
  and last segment carry `rounded-xs` on their outer corners. `aria-hidden`.
- _Track width is fixed_ per size, whatever the scale: 64 px on a card (56 px
  on a screen narrower than 360 px, where 64 px left „12 от 13“ too little room
  in a card — every card on the screen still draws the same track), 120 px
  on the product page. The filled length is therefore `value / max` of the same
  width on every product — the fraction is what is comparable, and the fraction
  is what the eye reads — while the number of segments shows which scale the
  brand used.
- _Numeral:_ `text-2xs tabular-nums text-ink-500` (card), `text-sm text-ink-900`
  (product page): "8 от 12". Always both numbers, always "от", never broken
  (`whitespace-nowrap`). On the product page the numeral may drop under the
  track where the facts table's value column is narrower than both (a 320 px
  phone).
- _Accessible text:_ `<span class="sr-only">Интензивност </span>` before the
  numeral, so it reads "Интензивност 8 от 12".

**Variants.**

- _Card:_ track 64 px, then the numeral, on one line, 16 px high.
- _Product page (facts table row "Интензивност"):_ the band word when the data
  has `strength` ("Слабо", "Средно", "Силно"), then the track at 120 px, then
  the numeral: `Силно  ▮▮▮▮▮▮▮▮▮▮▯▯  10 от 12`.

**States.**

- _No intensity in the data_ (true of about a fifth of the catalog): the
  component renders nothing. On a card the row keeps its 16 px height.
- _A value that does not parse:_ the raw text only, no track.
- _`value` greater than `max`:_ treated as unparsed.

**Filtering and sorting** use the band (`strength`: weak, medium, strong), never
the numeral.

**Phone.** Same sizes.

**Never.** The numeral without its maximum ("Интензивност 8"). A percentage, a
rescaled figure ("6,7 / 10") or stars. A track whose segment count is anything
other than the product's own `max`. A track whose width varies with the scale,
so that a 13-step track looks longer than a 5-step one. Colour that changes
with strength (no green-to-red). Sorting a listing by the raw numeral.

`components/catalog/product-card.tsx`. The unit the whole catalog is built

font-semibold text-sm text-ink-700`, normal case. Either part may be absent;

   `BrandPackLine` in `product-card.tsx`, shared with the wizard's result.

text-ink-900">` containing the one link of the card, to the product page

tabular-nums text-ink-300 line-through`, preceded by `<span
class="sr-only">Стара цена </span>`.

     Never "0,00 €".

text-pine-700`, the string from `toPerServingView()` — "0,35 € на чаша", or

   touched, 40 px from `lg`; `text-sm`, `relative z-10` so it sits above the

   - In the server's HTML, and for good without JavaScript, it is a link to the
     product page's `#order` (`productHref(locale, product, "#order")`), which
     offers the phone when there is no script. Once hydrated it is a `<button>` with the same
     classes, so nothing moves; a press adds one unit and the cart notice
     answers it.
   - `out_of_stock`: the control is replaced by a `secondary` link "Виж
     продукта" of the same size, so heights still match.

**States.**

- _Rest:_ no border, no shadow but the pack's contact shadow.
- _Hover and focus-within:_ the name gains `underline underline-offset-2`. On
  devices that hover, a cut-out pack rises 5 px while its shadow widens and
  softens (`.pack-lift`, 280 ms); a photograph on white does not move.
- _Focus:_ the ring is drawn on the name link and, separately, on the
  add-to-cart control. Two tab stops per card, in that order.
- _Loading:_ none. A card arrives with its page, rendered on the server; see
  "Loading".

**Equal heights.** Every row above has a fixed or minimum height and the price
block is pinned with `mt-auto`, so all cards in a grid row are the same height
and their prices and buttons line up, whatever is missing.

**Phone.** Two columns from 320 px up. At 390 px a card is about 173 px wide,
under 13rem: the control takes the full width under the price. Nothing is hidden on a phone: system, intensity and
price per cup are the reasons to shop here. The name clamps at three lines.

**Data the card needs** beyond today's `ProductCardView`: the resolved system
id, the parsed intensity, and `pricePerServing` with `servingsEstimated`.

**Never.** A description paragraph. More than one price per cup. A star, a
rating, a "bestseller" ribbon. A second link to the same product. A gold
button — twenty-four gold buttons are not an accent. A photograph on a tinted
or padded well (only a cut-out stands on the fading well). A hover zoom. A card whose height depends on which optional rows it has.

### Image placeholder

For a product with no photograph, or whose image URL is not on our own host.

**Anatomy.** Fills the well it replaces, same aspect ratio: `flex flex-col
items-center justify-center gap-2 bg-paper-sunken`.

- A line drawing of a coffee pack, 40% of the well's width, stroke 1.5 px in

- Beneath it: `<span class="text-2xs text-ink-500">Няма снимка</span>`. Omitted

### Brand logo

`BrandLogo` in `components/catalog/brand-logo.tsx`; the files in
`public/brand-logos/`, what a page needs to draw them in `content/brand-logos.ts`,
and where each came from in `content/brand-logo-provenance.ts` (imported by
tests only, so it never reaches the browser). A brand's own logo, shown
wherever the brand is named as a brand, sized for the place.

**Why it exists, and its limit.** A logo is the brand's trademark. We show it
for one reason: to identify the genuine product the shop sells. So it is the
brand's own file, checked against the mark on the packs in our photographs,
with that check written down beside it. It comes, in this order of
preference, from the brand's own website or press kit, the site of the company
that owns or makes the brand, the brand's own social media page, or Wikimedia
Commons; a logo library or another seller's copy only as a last resort, and
only if it matches the pack exactly (none is used today). A logo published
only on a white ground may have that white made transparent, exactly and with
nothing else changed, and a file may be scaled down; both are written down in
its provenance. It
identifies a _brand_ — the maker of a coffee — and never stands for a brewing
system: a system is named by the system badge, in text, even where the system
owner is also a brand we sell (Lavazza, Caffitaly).

**Anatomy.**

```
┌───────────── box ─────────────┐
│                               │       ┌──── box ────┐
│        [ LAVAZZA ]            │       │  ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓  │  dark ground:
│                               │       │  ▓ LOLLO ▓  │  ink-900 tile,
└───────────────────────────────┘       │  ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓  │  hugging the logo
                                        └─────────────┘
```

- _Image:_ a plain `<img>` from our own origin — not `next/image`, which would
  need `dangerouslyAllowSVG` for the SVG files. `alt` is the brand's display
  name; explicit `width` and `height` in whole pixels, so the box is known
  before the file arrives; `loading="lazy"` except on the brand page.
- _Box:_ the most room a placement gives a logo, fixed per placement (below).
  In a row of tiles the box is reserved whole and the logo centred in it, so
  every tile is the same size whatever it holds (so are a filter option's and
  a typeahead brand row's boxes, so the names after them line up); inline (the
  product page, the brand page, a card's brand line, a chip) the frame hugs the
  logo.
- _Dark tile_ (`ground: "dark"`): `bg-ink-900 rounded-sm` around the logo,
  padding per box, never filling the whole box.

**Boxes.**

| Size         | Box (w × h) | Dark-tile padding (x, y) | Where                                                                          |
| ------------ | ----------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `tile`       | 160 × 56    | 10, 8                    | Brands index, home page brand row.                                             |
| `header`     | 240 × 80    | 14, 10                   | Above the `h1` on a brand page.                                                |
| `line`       | 120 × 40    | 6, 2                     | The product page's brand line.                                                 |
| `suggestion` | 72 × 40     | 6, 2                     | Before the name in the search dropdown's brand rows.                           |
| `card`       | 88 × 40     | 6, 2                     | The brand line of a product card and of a wizard result.                       |
| `chip`       | 72 × 24     | 3, 2                     | Before the name in a brand chip (available to order); a typeahead product row. |

Forty pixels is the height at which every logo in the catalogue can be read,
the stacked lockups included (below), so every placement whose layout can give
it gets 40: the product page, the card and the typeahead's brand row (as tall as
a product row beside it). A
chip is 36 px tall and cannot; its 24 px box shows the five stacked lockups as
their name. The dark-tile padding in the 40 px boxes is 2 px above and below
because Lollo Caffè's and Vandino's lockups need the rest of the height.

Inside a container narrower than the box (a phone tile), the logo shrinks with
`max-width: 100%` and keeps its proportions. On a card's brand line the logo
also gives way to the pack size beside it (`shrink`), so on a 320 px phone
Biancaffè's wordmark narrows instead of pushing "500 г" out of the card.

**The optical rule.** Logos are sized by area, not by one height. At a common
height a square mark (illy, Julius Meinl, the Vergnano roundel) is a speck
beside a 4:1 wordmark (Lavazza), a quarter of its ink. Each logo instead gets
the same area — `AREA_SHARE` (0.75) of the box height squared — and its height
follows from its aspect ratio:

    height = √(0.75 × boxHeight² ÷ (width ÷ height))

So a square mark is about 0.87 of the box tall and a 4:1 wordmark half that and
twice as wide. Then, in order: no taller than the box and no shorter than
`MIN_HEIGHT` (12 px); no wider than the box, which wins over the minimum; and a
PNG is never drawn larger than its own pixels. A logo on a dark tile is fitted
to the room inside the tile's padding, so tile and logo together weigh about
what a logo on paper does. `test/brand-logo.test.ts` checks the rule and runs
every logo in the catalogue through every box.

**Legibility.** Some files are lockups whose name is a thin line under an
emblem (Bianchi, Lollo Caffè, Vandino), a brushed script under a large numeral
(3 Bourbons), or a pale gold name that fades at small sizes (Este). Each such
file records a `minHeight`, judged by eye at 1x: the smallest height at which
its name can still be read. A placement whose box would draw the logo below
that shows the name in text instead. Today that is the `chip` box for those
five brands: their active-filter chips, their chips in "Налични по поръчка" and
the brand part of their typeahead product rows.

**Grounds.** Most logos are drawn for light grounds and sit on the page's own
paper or a white tile. A logo the brand publishes only in white — Lollo Caffè,
Rema Caffè — sits on what it was made for, a dark tile in `ink-900`, the darkest
neutral; Rema's black pods agree, and Lollo's packs change colour by blend, so
no single colour of theirs is the brand's ground. Vandino's only logo is gold
drawn for a dark-green pack, about 2.1:1 on white, under the 3:1 a graphic
needs, so it sits on the same tile. Este's gold is about 2.6:1 on white too,
but every letter carries a near-black shadow and "COFFEE" is near-black, which
a dark tile would swallow, so Este sits on paper. The logo's own white on `ink-900` measures
17.36:1 (see the contrast table). Self-contained marks that carry their own
fill (illy's and Julius Meinl's red squares, the Vergnano roundel) sit on paper
as they are.

**Text fallback.** A brand with no logo — none found or none usable (Eurocaf,
Molini, each with the reason recorded), or one the sync added after the file
was written — shows its display name in the same box: `font-display
font-semibold text-ink-700`, `text-lg` in a tile and `text-sm` elsewhere,
clamped to two lines; centred in a reserved box, in normal case on a card's
brand line. A row of tiles stays even, and a card's 40 px brand line keeps its
height. Where the name is already printed next to the logo, the placement
renders nothing instead (a brand page `h1`, a chip) or keeps an empty box so
names line up (a typeahead brand row).

**Where it appears.**

| Place                          | Size         | Notes                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------ | ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Brands index (`routes.brands`) | `tile`       | Grid of tiles: logo, then the name (`h2`) and the product count in text. The logo takes `alt=""` there — the name follows inside the same link — and the fallback is `aria-hidden`. |
| Brand page header              | `header`     | Logo above the `h1`, eager; the `h1` stays text. No logo: nothing.                                                                                                                  |
| Home page, brand row           | `tile`       | Logo tiles, one link each to the brand page; `alt` is the name and the count follows in `sr-only`.                                                                                  |
| Product page, brand line       | `line`       | A small logo linking to the brand page, beside the system badge; `alt` is the name, so it is the link's name. Not legible or absent: the Label-style text link.                     |
| Search typeahead, brand rows   | `suggestion` | Before the name, `alt=""` (the name follows); an empty box with no logo.                                                                                                            |
| Product card, brand line       | `card`       | Wherever a card is drawn: listings, search, brand pages, landings, related products, home shelves. In place of the name, so `alt` is the name. No logo: the name in its place.      |
| Wizard result, brand line      | `card`       | As on the product card.                                                                                                                                                             |
| Brands index, "by order" chips | `chip`       | The brands with no product in stock, as chips: before the name, `alt=""`; not legible at 24 px: the name alone.                                                                     |
| Search typeahead, product rows | `chip`       | After the system badge, `alt` the name, then "·" and the pack size. Not legible: the name in text, as before.                                                                       |
| Structured data                | —            | `logo`, as an absolute URL, on the `Brand` in the Product and brand-page JSON-LD when a logo exists.                                                                                |

**Where it does not.** Beside a brewing system's name: the system chips,
the system filter options and chips, the system badge and the brand page's
"Сравнете с останалите марки" shelves carry the system's icon, even
for Caffitaly and Lavazza, which are both (see "Never"). Breadcrumbs and
product names, where the brand is a word in a sentence-like row and the logo
is a few pixels away. Running text (the brands index introduction, a brand
summary sentence). The coffee-machine brands (`za-kafemashina`): they make
the machines a customer already owns, are not brands the shop sells, and have
no logo here. Anywhere a logo would come out smaller than it can be read, it is not
shown.

**Never.** Recolour, invert, crop, stretch, redraw, outline or trace a logo.
Combine two logos, or a logo with our own mark, or set text over one. Put a
logo on a ground it was not drawn for (a white-only logo on paper, a dark logo
on the dark tile). Upscale a PNG past its own pixels. A logo from the shop
this one mirrors, an image search's thumbnail, or a copy that does not match
the pack exactly, or one whose provenance is not recorded. A logo for a brewing system or as a compatibility
claim. A logo in place of the brand's name where a screen reader needs it. A
logo as a product's photograph.

- _Right:_ `NewsletterForm` (`components/forms/newsletter-form.tsx`), max
  `max-w-xl`: the email field (Inputs, `min-h-12`, `type="email"`,
  `required`, `autoComplete="email"`, a visually hidden label „Имейл адрес“),
  and a `primary` button „Абонирай ме“. Side by side from `@md` of form

  in `text-xs text-ink-500`, tied to the field by `aria-describedby`: „Можете
  да се отпишете по всяко време. Какво пазим и защо — в политиката за

**Ground.** `paper-raised`, a step lighter than the page, between hairlines.
On the home page it follows the Vending Zone band (`espresso-900`): it has that
band's anatomy — heading and sentence left, the action right, stacked on a
phone — on the opposite ground, so the two read as one family without being
one thing twice, and two dark bands never stack. It is not `paper-sunken`,
which would read as part of the footer under it.

**Behaviour.** A real form posting to a server action, so it works without
JavaScript. The honeypot field, the rate limit and the stored consent are the
server's (`lib/forms/actions.ts`); the consent source stays `footer` („Формата
в долната част на сайта“ in the admin panel), which it still is. While it
sends, the button is disabled (a second click sends nothing), `aria-busy`,
and says „Изпраща се…“ at the width it had. Success replaces the form with a
`role="status"` line in `text-base text-ink-900` on `paper-raised` in a
`border-pine-500` box; an error shows under the field in `text-critical`.
With JavaScript the address is checked with `newsletterSchema` when the button
is pressed, and nothing is sent until it passes; without, the server's answer
says the same thing in the same place and keeps the address in the field
(Inputs, "Validation"). The analytics event `newsletter_submitted` records the
outcome of what reached the server.

**Where it is not.** A page renders `<NewsletterBandOff />` (a hidden
`data-newsletter-off` marker; `globals.css` hides the band with
`body:has([data-newsletter-off])`, on the server's own HTML, with no script
and nothing that moves after load):

- the unsubscribe page — whoever is on it is leaving the list;
- the contact page, the Vending Zone and the consumables page — each ends on
  an enquiry form that already asks for this, as a check box, right above
  where the band would be;
- the 404 — a visitor who followed a dead link needs a way on, not an offer.

`components/layout/site-footer.tsx`. `bg-paper-sunken`, `border-t

  Nespresso", "Кафе дози ESE"), never the bare system name, because down here

hover:underline`, `lg:min-h-7`.

| Width       | Tier 2                                                                                                     |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Phone       | Each group a `<details>`, closed: a 48 px row with the heading and a chevron that turns.                   |
| `md`        | `grid-cols-3`: Магазин across all three, its links in three columns; Помощ and Правна информация under it. |

### Breadcrumbs

`Breadcrumbs` in `primitives.tsx`, the first thing under the header on every

- _Below `lg`:_ one line that scrolls sideways, running to the screen's

### Loading

**There is no route-level loading state.** No `loading.tsx` exists under
`app/`, and no Suspense boundary sits above a page. A boundary above the page
lets Next send the shell before the page has decided what it is, and from then
on the page can no longer answer with a status. These pages answer 308 for a
product's previous slug, a brand's stored slug and a category's stored slug,
and 404 for what does not exist; streamed after a shell, each of those becomes
a 200 that JavaScript corrects. The pages are cached (`revalidate`) and render
whole, so there is little to wait for.

What a visitor sees while something is pending:

- _The search field._ The interactive field sits in a Suspense boundary of
  its own, inside the header and the search page, never above a page. Its
  fallback is `SearchFieldFallback`: the same box, at the same size, as a real
  `GET` form that already works. Not a skeleton.
- _A submitting form._ The button goes to the pending state: `disabled`,
  `aria-busy`, and a label that says what is happening ("Изпраща се…"). It
  keeps its width. The result arrives in the form's `aria-live` line.
- _The cart page._ The cart is in the browser and its prices come from the
  server, so the page draws the skeleton of its lines (`aria-busy`, one
  `sr-only` „Зарежда се…“) until both are in. It is the one client-side region
  that does; see "Cart page".
- _A card's „Добави“ and the header's count._ Both are in the server's HTML
  (the control as a link, the count absent) and change in place once the
  page hydrates; the count is a badge over the icon, outside the flow.

**Skeletons.** `.skeleton` (from `globals.css`) is `paper-sunken`,
`rounded-sm`, with a 1.6 s opacity pulse that stops under reduced motion. No
component draws one today. If a client-side region ever needs one, it
reproduces the boxes of what it replaces so nothing moves when the content
arrives, the region carries `aria-busy="true"` and one `<span class="sr-only"
role="status">Зарежда се…</span>`, and it never stands in for a whole page.

**Never.** A `loading.tsx`, or a Suspense boundary above a page that can
redirect or 404. A full-page spinner. A skeleton whose boxes differ in size
from the content. Shimmer gradients sweeping across the page.

`components/not-found-body.tsx`, returned with a real 404 status and rendered

the shop's frame (header, search, footer) and always in Bulgarian; and
`app/(site)/[lang]/not-found.tsx`, for a `notFound()` thrown inside a page.

1. "404" — `font-display text-5xl font-semibold text-pine-500`, `aria-hidden`,

2. `h1`, Page title: "Тази страница я няма".
3. `text-base text-ink-500`, max 28rem: "Може адресът да е сгрешен или
   продуктът да е спрян."
4. The search field, `max-w-md`.
5. Actions: `primary` "Към началната страница"; `secondary` "Намери по машина".
6. `text-sm text-ink-500`: "Търсите нещо конкретно? " and a link to contact.

### Focus and skip link

- Every interactive element shows the global ring on `:focus-visible`: 2 px
  `pine-700`, 2 px offset (8.15:1 or better on every paper tone). Inside a
  `.on-pine` container the ring is `gold-300` (8.09:1). Every `pine-900` band
  carries `.on-pine`.
- `outline: none` is not written anywhere without an equal replacement.
- `.skip-link` is the first focusable element and jumps to `main`.
- Focus order follows reading order. A dialog traps focus and returns it.

### Mail

`src/emails/`, React Email, rendered to an HTML string by `renderEmail` and
sent with a plain-text twin. Every message the shop sends is drawn in
`EmailLayout`: the order confirmation, the owner's notifications, a reply from
the panel and the newsletter. The inbound forward is the exception — a band
over a stranger's own message, in the same palette.

**Anatomy.** `paper` page; a 600 px `paper-raised` sheet with a `line` hairline,
`rounded-md` and a 4 px `pine-900` top edge; the lockup at 200 px
(`public/email-logo.png`, flattened on white so a client that darkens the
message leaves it legible); content; then, under the sheet in `text-xs
ink-500`, the shop's name, phone, address, hours and legal line — and, in a
newsletter, why the reader gets it and the link to leave, first.

**Type and colour.** Literata → Georgia for headings, the system sans for the
rest (no web font loads in mail). Sizes from the scale, 11 px floor. Only the
pairings in "Measured contrast": ink on paper tones, `pine-700` links, `paper`
on `pine-900` buttons, `pine-900` on `pine-100` and ink on `caution-100`
callouts. No gold, no clay.

**Blocks.** `Heading`, `Paragraph`, `Button` (primary pine, secondary
outlined), `Divider`, `KeyValueRows`, `OrderLines` (two columns: name with
"2 бр. × 19,90 €" under it, the line total right; the sum and what it leaves
out), `Callout`, `ImageBlock` (alt required), `AttachmentList`, `Signature`.

**Never.** A relative URL, a `<style>` block, a colour not in `theme.ts`, an
image that carries meaning without its text, HTML from the newsletter
composer (its body is a Markdown subset, `emails/markdown.ts`, drawn by these
components). `pnpm --filter @catalog/web email:preview` renders every template
to files; `test/emails.test.ts` checks the rules above on the rendered HTML.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- **Do** start from the machine: system and machine finder before brand,
  everywhere there is a choice of order.
- **Do** put the system's name on every capsule and pod, in text.
- **Do** show an intensity as "8 от 12", with a track of twelve segments.
- **Do** put the price per cup, or per kilogram, directly under the price.
- **Do** mark a figure derived from weight with "≈".
- **Do** stand a cut-out pack on its well with its contact shadow, and leave a
  pack that has no cut-out on pure white, unframed.
- **Do** keep a page's likely LCP element still: it may move, never fade.
- **Do** draw what decorates — beans, steam, cups — in the drawing tones.
- **Do** show a brand's own logo wherever the brand is named as a brand, at
  a size it can be read at, and its name in text where it cannot.
- **Do** keep one gold control per viewport, on pine, on espresso or on a white
  panel.
- **Do** keep clay for a price that went down.
- **Do** reserve the box of anything optional or late, so nothing shifts.
- **Do** write every control as a link or a form first.
- **Do** omit a section, a row or a fact that has no data.
- **Do** set every comparable figure in tabular numerals with its unit.
- **Do** add a row to the contrast table before using a new colour pairing.

### Don't

- **Don't** use gold as text on a paper tone, or the accent button on `paper`
  or `paper-sunken`.
- **Don't** use a system colour without the system's name beside it.
- **Don't** show an intensity numeral without its maximum, rescale it, or sort
  by it.
- **Don't** use clay for cautions, hovers, taglines or decoration.
- **Don't** use `critical` outside a form or a destructive action.
- **Don't** lighten text with opacity. Use the next ink step.
- **Don't** round a control or a container into a pill or a circle.
- **Don't** put a shadow on anything at rest but a pack's contact shadow, or a
  bordered panel inside a bordered panel.
- **Don't** zoom or slide anything on hover. Only a pack lifts, and only where
  there is hover.
- **Don't** add a carousel, a countdown, a pop-up or a dismissible banner.
- **Don't** use stock, lifestyle or generated photography, a photograph of
  beans or cups, a system owner's logo as the mark of a system, or another
  product's photograph as a stand-in.
- **Don't** load an animation library, animate a layout property, or start a
  loop that runs while nobody can see it.
- **Don't** use a drawing tone for text, a border or a meaning.
- **Don't** recolour, crop, stretch or combine a brand's logo, or show one
  smaller than it can be read (see "Brand logo").
- **Don't** print a rating, a review, a "bestseller" label or any fact about a
  coffee that the record does not hold.
- **Don't** show "0,00 €", "—" or "няма данни" for a missing value. Say "Цена
  при запитване", or omit the row.
- **Don't** set a sentence, a system name or a brand name in uppercase.
- **Don't** set a form field below 16 px, or any text below 11 px.
- **Don't** build a control that needs JavaScript to work.

`test/contrast.test.ts`; there is no automated WCAG audit.

What the specification already does to meet them:

- **LCP.** One `priority` image per page: the first hero packshot on the home
  page, the first row of cards on a listing, the main gallery image on a
  product page. Every other image is lazy. Images come from our own host
  through `next/image` with a `sizes` attribute. Two font families, loaded with
  `next/font` and `display: swap`. No motion script above the fold (see "The
  Web Vitals rules"); the client components there are the header's controls,
  the image wrapper and, on a product page, the gallery and the cart panel.
- **CLS.** Every image well has an aspect ratio. Optional card rows keep their
  height. The announcement bar cannot be dismissed. The search field's
  fallback is the field's own box. Buttons keep their width while pending.
- **Accessibility.** The contrast table above; one `h1` and ordered headings;
  labelled fields with tied errors; a visible focus ring on every ground;
  targets of at least 44 px below `lg` ("The Touch Target Rule"), and from
  `lg` at least 24 by 24 px, 36 px or more for chips and filter rows and 40 px
  or more for card and toolbar controls; no meaning by colour alone; motion
  off under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
